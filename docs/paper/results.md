## Area of spontaneous release events
  After preprocessing, inspection of the image sequences revealed fluorescent emissions that occurred without external input, which are *termed *here spontaneous release events (SREs). The location, size and frequency of these events was quantified. A SRE was defined by the occurrence increased intensity above 2 sigma in a set of contiguous pixels.
Sets < 5000 pixels were excluded from the analysis.
  In any one frame, multiple release events could be observed (See Fig 1a) and some were repeated in late frames. Observations of higher magnification often truncated the release area because of the limited frame sizes with 60x and 40x objectives. Observation with 10x objective permitted observation of the entire extent of most events. The area of these events was distributed over a range from x um to y um (Fig 1b - d)

## Recurrence of spontaneous release events
  Observation of successive frames indicates that some events recur in the same location in multiple frames. Some occur in consecutive frames, and some recur in approximately the same region after intervals of no release events in these locations.
  The temporal reliability of the SRE suggest there may be a structural basis to their contiguity observed. The nature of the connecting structure could be the axonal field of individual neurons, or a connected group of neurons.
  Recurrent events in general were not seen to be identical in terms of their active pixels. Thus, there is a need to establish identity. If the pixels of SREs belong to the same structure, there should be a correlation among the pixel activity over time.
  Using the footprints of the SREs as ROIs, the time series of the mean activity within each SRE could be measured. If the time series are highly correlated, it implies the SREs share the same identity. For example, Fig 2a shows 3 SREs from a frame sequence. The time series *by these SREs is shown in Fig 2b-d. It can be seen that the blue and orange traces are highly correlated (r^2=0.992, pearson's correlation) while the correlation with the green SRE is low (0.139). This suggests that the first two are part of the same structure whereas the 3rd has a distinct identity.

  Having found the SREs, the next step is to determine the extent of overlap among SREs. At one extreme, all SRE might be tiled, i.e., have no overlap. At the other extreme SREs might all overlap with each other. To obtain a measure of the degree of overlap of SREs, maps of all SREs observed were prepared. (Fig 3)
